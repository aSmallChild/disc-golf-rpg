# Tilt and Meltdowns

## User's notes
- There is a risk of a **mental break**: you get really angry, and the anger needs **time to cool off**.
- Asked for a better term than "anger".
- The chalk bag clap manages anger after a bad putt (from `gear-chalk-bag.md`).

## Terms *(suggestion)*
- **Tilt**: the meter. Real disc golfers (and poker players) already say "on tilt", and it's light-hearted.
- **Meltdown**: the mental break, when tilt hits the top.
- **Cooling off**: the recovery period after a meltdown.

Other options for the break: "losing the plot", "red mist", "spitting the dummy".

## How it relates to nerves *(suggestion)*
- **Nerves** = pressure **before** a shot (anticipation). Raised by stakes and expectation.
- **Tilt** = frustration **after** bad shots (reaction). Raised by bad outcomes, especially unlucky ones.
- **Composure** (attribute) resists both. See `character-stats.md`.
- The two can feed each other: a high-nerve miss raises tilt more than a casual miss.

## Suggestions
- *(suggestion)* **Raises tilt**: bad kicks, OB, missed short putts, lost discs, gear backfires (chalk bag bursts), a card-mate's lucky shot.
- *(suggestion)* **Warning stage** before a meltdown ("steaming"): the character's animation and the UI change, so the player can act before it's too late.
- *(suggestion)* **Meltdown effects** (comedic, but they hurt): the disc gets thrown into the bushes or the lake (risk of losing it), the bag gets kicked over, a time violation from ranting, big accuracy and Read penalties.
- *(suggestion)* **Cooling off**: lasts a number of shots or holes, not real seconds. Accuracy and decision quality are reduced, and some shot types are locked (no tight lines, nothing that needs patience).
- *(suggestion)* **Ways to cool off faster**: chalk clap, a walk to the next tee, a snack, a calming skill, safe shots. The player always has something to do.
- *(suggestion)* Skills like "Walk It Off" (faster cooling), "Short Memory" (tilt from a bad shot fades after one shot), and "Controlled Rage" (some tilt adds distance on drives).

## Critique
- **Fun**: meltdowns are memorable, funny, and very "disc golf". They fit the harsh "low skill, bad luck" tone.
- **Risk: losing agency.** The player already doesn't control the throw. If a meltdown also takes away choices, it feels like the game is playing itself. Keep player choices during cooling off: a smaller set of shots, but still decisions.
- **Risk: frustration loop.** Bad luck causes tilt, which causes more bad shots. The warning stage, cooling-off actions, and a cap stop it from snowballing.
- **Risk: double punishment.** A bad shot already costs a stroke. Tilt should mostly matter if you get several bad shots in a row, not after every single one.
- **Right challenge?** Yes, if it's visible and manageable. Recognising you're steaming and choosing a safe shot or a cool-down action is a good decision challenge.
