# Skills Follow Disc Golf Scoring

User's notes:
- Where it applies, skills use disc golf scoring. Good skill is a **negative (good) score** and bad skill is a positive (bad) score.
- Skills don't start at 0. They **start in the bad range**.
- The starting game must not be impossible. It should make it obvious that you are a bad player who badly needs skill (and gear) to improve outcomes.
- Example: low luck increases the chance that the chalk bag breaks and blinds the player, so they need a time extension.

*(suggestions)*
- Use golf terms for the scale, for example: **Triple bogey (+3), Double bogey (+2), Bogey (+1), Par (0), Birdie (-1), Eagle (-2), Albatross (-3), Ace**. Skill levels could have names like "Bogey Chalker" or "Eagle Retriever".
- At bad levels (above par), gear can **backfire** (chalk bag bursts, retriever snaps). At par it's neutral. Below par it gives bonuses, and the backfire chance fades to nearly zero.
- New players get a low floor: backfires are comedic and cost time, strokes, or tilt, but rarely a lost disc. That keeps the early game rough but survivable.
- The roll breakdown (`skill-feedback.md`) can show it this way: "Chalk Bag: +2 (Double bogey). 18% chance of mishap".
